The provided bar chart compares both residents' purchasing and drinking habits ==regarding== coffee and tea across five Australian cities (Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Adelaide, and Hobart) in the previous four weeks.

==Overall==, going to a cafe took the dominant position across the majority of cities, with Adelaide being the sole exception. In contrast, buying fresh coffee consistently ranked the lowest across all five locations, while at least half of the population in each city maintained one of these habits.

In Sydney and Melbourne, the majority of people regarded cafes as the best choice for coffee/tea consumption, with the corresponding figures being 61% in Sydney and 63% in Melbourne. A more significant proportion of residents in Melbourne (47%) chose to buy instant coffee, compared to the percentage of those in Sydney showing a similar preference (45.2%). In contrast, those who preferred to buy fresh coffee constituted a slightly larger part of Sydney's residents (44%) than the percentage in Melbourne (42%).

A cafe remained people's priority for coffee/tea in the remaining three cities, except in Adelaide, where people showed marginally more interest in buying instant coffee (50%) rather than going to a cafe (49.5%). In Brisbane and Adelaide, the percentages of residents who were inclined to buy fresh coffee were almost equivalent, both under 35%, 3% lower than a similarly low Hobart. People in the three cities who favored instant coffee occupied at least half of the whole population, with respective figures being 53% (Brisbane), 50% (Adelaide), and 54% (Hobart).

# 家庭花销

![[20. resource/07. writing/02. 小作文/c. 柱状图/picture/柱状图_习题_家庭花销.png]]

The two bar charts compare the proportions of household spending on food and other goods across three European nations (France, Germany, and England) between 1998 and 2008.

==Overall==, expenditure in both categories saw an upward trend across all three countries over the ten-year period. In addition, France experienced the most dramatic growth in both sectors to register the highest shares in 2008, whereas England consistently allocated the smallest percentages to food throughout the timeframe.

==Regarding== spending on food, German households allocated a quarter of their budgets to this category in 1998, surpassing France (==around== 15%) and England (10%). By 2008, however, the proportion for France had surged to 40%, overtaking Germany where the figure climbed marginally to 30%. England also recorded a moderate rise to ==approximately== 15%, remaining the lowest among the three countries.

A comparable pattern was evident in expenditure on other goods, where Germany initially led with about 17% in 1998, compared to roughly 9% in France and 8% in England. Over the subsequent decade, household spending in France and England experienced substantial increases, soaring to 40% and 35% respectively. In contrast, Germany witnessed a comparatively modest growth, rising to roughly 24% and falling behind its two ==counterparts== by the end of the period.
