**Speaker**: Today I'm going to talk about something I hope you will find amusing because the topic is laughter. Despite its prominence in daily life, there has been little research on laughter. So after I became interested in this subject, I started off by having three of my psychology students conduct an observational survey as a component of the course which was designed to focus on the ==methodology== used in this field. The students and I observed twelve hundred people laughing spontaneously in their day-to-day worlds, from the university library to the city streets. Whenever we heard laughter, we noted down information about the exchange.

**Speaker**: As to our findings, well, we usually think of laughter coming from an audience after something funny has been said by a single speaker. But contrary to our expectations, the speakers we observed laughed about fifty percent more often than their audiences. Our observations also showed that simple everyday comments like, "Where have you been?" are far more likely to precede laughter than a ==joke== is. In fact, only ten to twenty percent of the laughter episodes we witnessed followed anything actually funny. The gender difference in laughter is also striking. In our twelve hundred case studies, we found that while everyone laughed a lot, ==females== laugh one hundred and twenty-six percent more often than their male counterparts. But men seem to be the main instigators of humor across cultures. I also had students in my classes note down in a ==diary== instances when they themselves laughed. When they analyzed the results, they found that laughter for them was thirty times more frequent in social situations than when they are alone. This suggests that laughter is a ==signal== we send to others.

**Speaker**: Now, moving on from this research to some more general observations, what do we know about the phenomenon of laughter and its psychological features? Oftentimes, if one person laughs, those around that person will also laugh even if they didn't hear or see what caused the laughter. Consider the extraordinary outbreak of laughter at one boarding school some years ago. The symptoms appeared when three students started laughing and couldn't stop. The symptoms rapidly spread, eventually affecting ninety-five students and forcing the school to close until the laughing stopped. Quite an amazing tale, I think you'll agree. What part of the brain controls laughter? Well, when a speaker laughs, the laugh usually occurs at the ==end== of sentences or phrases rather than being randomly scattered through the sentence. This suggests that different regions of the brain are involved in speech, which is largely based on cognition and laughter, which is based on ==emotion==.

**Speaker**: The physiological effects of laughter are incompletely documented, and their medical benefits are even less certain. But it is known that in many people laughter does, at least temporarily, raise blood pressure. Results of other studies have proved less definite. At least one study reported that comedy and laughter increased the levels of certain chemicals in the urine that were known to be associated with ==stress==. On the other hand, other research in the US reported that laughter reduced these same chemical levels, which indicated the exact opposite effect. So it is far from certain what the effects of laughter are on these particular chemicals, and thus the ultimate effects of laughter in this regard. However, one of laughter's most promising applications, which has been rather extensively noted experimentally, is the reduction of the extent to which we feel ==pain==. One American university study found that this was tolerated better by subjects who laughed at the films of certain comedians than by other subjects who did not watch the films. In another American study, patients who had undergone surgery and then watched comedy films requested fewer aspirin and tranquilizers than others who had viewed serious dramatic films. And science has long understood that laughter causes the prefrontal cortex of the brain to release a certain ==hormone== which makes the person feel happy, although the exact mechanism is not completely understood, nor is its effect. Is laughter universal among humans?

# 错题记录

the course aimed to study the \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ used in the survey of laughter

> 正确: methodology。原文: I started off by having three of my psychology students conduct an observational survey as a component of the course which was designed to focus on the methodology used in this field.
> 错误: mediology
> 常见陷阱: 听音偏差
> 直接原因: 听到了方法论的发音, 但拼写错误写成了不存在的 mediology。
> 破局法则: 完成题目: 学科常用长词落笔后按音节 method-ology 核对拼写。

everyday comments can cause more laughter than a \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

> 正确: joke。原文: Our observations also showed that simple everyday comments like, "Where have you been?" are far more likely to precede laughter than a joke is.
> 错误: joker
> 常见陷阱: 听音偏差
> 直接原因: 定位到了 than 后面的比较对象, 但把 joke 听写成了指人的 joker, 不符合句意。
> 破局法则: 完成题目: 落笔后结合句意检查词义, 引起笑声的是 joke(笑话) 而非 joker(爱开玩笑的人)。

> 直接原因: 听到了 females, 但漏写了复数词尾 s; 题干 tend 为复数动词, 要求主语必须为复数 females。
> 破局法则: 完成题目: 根据题干动词 tend 预判主语单复数, 复核词尾 s。
