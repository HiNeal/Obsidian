**Lecturer**: Thanks for coming, Tom and Sophie. So, you're doing a project on jeans with a multimedia display and a written report.

**Student**: Yeah.

**Lecturer**: I've had a look at the plans for the display. Let's start with that. So you've got a map to show where the name jeans comes from.

**Student**: Yes. In fact, before we started the project, we both assumed it was somewhere in France, because that's where the jeans fabric, the ==denim==, was first made. But it's actually from the Italian city of Genoa, where they made a similar fabric in the sixteenth century that was used for trousers worn by sailors. But nowadays, most people associate jeans with rural America, the sorts of thing that farmers and workmen wore in the nineteenth century and afterwards.

**Lecturer**: Okay. Then you're going to show how jeans became increasingly popular in the eighteen-forties, and you're going to use a short clip from a cowboy movie for that. So what made you decide to do that?

**Student**: Well, the movie's very recent, but in fact, the costume designs are accurate for the period, and it'll give our audience a feel for the sort of setting where jeans were worn at that time in America. And anyway, nearly everyone enjoys cowboy movies. [laughs]

**Lecturer**: Okay. Now, I'm glad you've included something about the pockets of the jeans, because this was certainly something that attracted the original customers.

**Tom**: Yeah. Nowadays, manufacturers use all sorts of fancy stitches and designs on jeans pockets. But at first, people were more interested in the utilitarian side of things and the way the manufacturer used copper ==rivets==, like little metal buttons, to make sure the pockets didn't get torn off. They used them on both the jeans made from blue denim and those made of something called cotton duck, which was actually brown.

**Student**: Right. And we went to the museum because we wanted to see if they had something from eighteen seventy-three, because that was the year when Levi Strauss took out a patent on his design for jeans. And we were really lucky because they had a ==pair== from that exact same year. They couldn't let us take them away, of course, but they did let us take a photo of them.

**Lecturer**: Brilliant. Now, in your display, you're planning to have a wall with thirty pairs of jeans hung on it. The wall's titled The Attraction of Jeans. What exactly is their most significant attraction?

**Student**: Well, for the wall hangings, we'll use our own jeans and those of our friends and family. We've got some going back to the sixties. There's never been a time since then when they've been out of ==style==. That's really the thing about jeans. And although they're so durable, that's not what people think about these days. And the main attraction's certainly not price, because they can be extremely expensive.

**Lecturer**: And then you've got a section in your display about designer labels.

**Student**: And for that, we'd thought of interviewing the other students about their opinions, but that wouldn't be very visually interesting. So then we considered cutting out ==ads== for designer jeans from magazines, and we found some really good ones. We'd thought of looking on the Internet for quotes from celebrities about their favorite designer labels, and we found some, but they didn't really say much, so we abandoned that.

**Lecturer**: Okay. Uh, now let's look at possible improvements to the draft of your written report. Your section on history is very interesting, and I think you've illustrated your points more than adequately with relevant examples. But though your emphasis is on the past, and rightly so, I'd like you to use your knowledge of the past to say where you think the whole thing might go. Will jeans still be bestsellers, say, in ten years' time?

**Student**: Okay, we'll see what we can come up with. Now, the next section we called Variety. We've got a lot about different brands and styles of jeans. But I think we need to add something about using jeans for work because they're so comfortable and ==tough==. They're not just for style by a long shot. For example, even my dad wears them around the house all the time.

**Lecturer**: I agree. That would be a good aspect to add. Now, you've also got a section called Manufacturing. I thought that was quite good, but I must admit, I thought some of the technical stuff about making the cloth could have been more clearly expressed.

**Student**: Hmm. I think it would be much easier if we had some step-by-step ==drawings== to go with the text. I'm not bad at that, so I'll do that.

**Lecturer**: Your section on marketing seems good, especially the pictures you've included as examples. But you seem to deal almost exclusively with advertising in America and Europe. And in fact, marketing jeans is a huge phenomenon covering all corners of the earth. And your report should reflect that, shouldn't it?

**Student**: You're right. Actually, I found a couple of useful Internet articles that argue just that, so we can use those.
