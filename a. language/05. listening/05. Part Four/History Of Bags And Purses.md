The oldest bag preserved in the history is from the Tyrolean Iceman. This nickname was given to a well-preserved natural mummy. The man reportedly lived between 3400 and 3100 BC.

In the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries, bags were called ==purses==, ==exclusively== carried ==coins==. These purses were essential as pockets in clothing were not developed just yet. In the fourteenth century, and without pockets in clothing, a purse was attached to the strings from the ==waist==.

From the sixteenth century onward, ==priests== often wore their purses, a hook with chains to which small things could be attached, such as keys, knife cases, ==scissors==, and ==sewing== tools. Since purses were often crafted from precious metals, they were also considered as a symbol of ==status==. The design and ==accessories== evolved in the course of centuries, but it wasn't until the beginning of the twentieth century that the handbag finally replaced it.

Bags and purses came in a ==variety== of designs for a number of purposes, such as bags with ==clasps==, leather pouches. These were all worn attached to the ==belt==. The introduction of pockets towards the end of the sixteenth century meant that men's bags slowly disappeared in the course of seventeenth century. From then on, bags belonged to almost exclusively to the women's domain.

In the seventeenth and eighteenth century, women's clothing was so large that one or two bags or pockets could easily be hidden underneath the ==skirt==. Such pockets were usually worn in ==pairs==, one hanging from each ==hip==. ==Hence== the name thigh pockets. Thigh pockets remained fashionable for most of the nineteenth century.

The dark times of the medieval age gave way to manufacturing a curious portraits on purses. Medieval purses were not only used as coin purse, they also were closely connected with ==marriage==. As a result, they commonly showed love stories. Interestingly enough, this connection is deduced from connection between the handbag and ==fertility==.

Bags ==altered== to serve their uses to ==optimize== their wearer's way of life. At least such was the case with sweetie bags, which are the bags that filled with sweet-smelling material. In a time when personal hygiene was poor, sweet bags were stuffed with sweet-smelling ==spices== to get rid of bad smelling. For example, lavenders or perfumed balls of cotton. In the long run, sweet bags were also stored with clothes and linens used for ==aromatherapy==.

The bag continued to be highly functional and had many different nicknames and uses. Purses were often used as decorative package for ==gifts== such as money, perfume, or jewels. ==Notably==, towards the end of the century, purses became more sophisticated, ==evolving== from simple drawstring design to more innovative shapes with dynamic materials. ==Seemingly==, texture and manufacturing attributes were the only details left to be ==stylized==.

For instance, the 1930s saw the Art Deco style arrival. This particular style specifically highlighted abstraction, just like any time period which experiences new industrial materials. The bag integrates them as well as within its design. In this particular case, plastic and ==zippers== were introduced to the bag.

Once the 1960s began, the bag experienced a self-expression as if being dynamic and ==versatile== wasn't already be part of bag's characteristics. The bag experienced a brave ==awakening==, specifically colorful patterns, and flower power introduced energy into the fabric of the bag. The end of the 1960s saw the shoulder bag return, and with it came lots of buckles, all of which indicated that women and their bags were ultimately fit and ready for the beginning age of ==feminism==.

All in all, the 1980s saw an ==expansion== in active consumption. The bag quickly changed to ==accommodate== ==purchasing== power, providing logos to indicate the ==priorities== of the owner. For instance, ==sports== bags such as Nike bags matched athletic shoes were most popular at this time. As a result, book bags and even the ==briefcase== specifically featured a pocket which were exclusively created to hold this new invention.
