Hello, everyone. My presentation today is about the ==cave== paintings produced by prehistoric artists about thirty thousand years ago, and in particular, the symbols that appear in or near these paintings. These symbols are found deep in caves in many countries around the world, but the ones I'm going to talk about today are mainly European. Firstly, a little about the most common symbols. The symbols that have been discovered are of different shapes and ==sizes==. Some are easily ==recognizable== as they're quite simple. The most basic are just a series of dots, but they may also include forms such as the ==triangle==. However, other symbols are more complex. A case in point is the ==wavy== line. This is sometimes used on its own, but the same wavy line is also used as part of a larger picture of a ==horse==. So we think this is what the wavy line represents.

One very important symbol is what's called the hand symbol. This ==frequently== appears on the walls of caves. I'll talk about it in a bit more detail. Firstly, how it was made. The paint itself was made up from a ==variety== of things that cave artists would have had access to. They had fire, so charcoal was one ingredient. Another was the ==fat== from the animals they would have ==hunted==, and lastly, the saliva from their own mouths.

Then there's the question of how they actually applied the ==runny== paint onto the walls of the cave. It certainly wouldn't have been easy. They would have had to use something ==hollow==. In fact, it's likely to have looked like a ==straw==, you know, like the kind we use for drinking nowadays. They'd have sucked the paint up into it, then blown it out. Researchers have recently recreated both the paint and the processes used to make the hand symbols. The artist would have first put his hand flat against the wall, then blown the paint onto the wall, creating an outline of the hand. The researchers who've tried to recreate the process have said that not only is it a difficult technique to master, but also that a strange ==noise== is produced. It also led them to feel light-headed, so we can only imagine that these cave artists felt the same.

I'm sure you'll agree that these symbols are really interesting, but they're also of ==vital== importance. Researchers have now identified a total of twenty-six symbols commonly used in France and Spain. We only have this knowledge thanks to a database that's been created and which is being added to all the time. It clearly demonstrates that many symbols are often used in particular ==combinations==, even if they're in different places. This is significant as it looks like different ==tribes== of cave people who lived and traveled through the area at that time would have seen these markings as well as adding to them. Although researchers don't believe that these symbols are a language as such, they are happy to call them a type of ==code==. There is evidence to support this idea.

One sequence of five symbols is particularly common in caves in Spain.

Exactly the same sequence of five symbols has been found carved into a ==necklace== made from the teeth of a red ==deer==. So it appears that this combination of symbols may have had a special meaning.

Of course, many of these explanations are theory as opposed to hard facts, which is why there is disagreement among researchers about the ==significance== of what we are seeing. One theory is called The Great Leap Forward.

The idea is that around thirty-five thousand years ago, there was a ==sudden increase== in creativity which led to the invention of the ==boat==, among other things. However, not everyone agrees. In fact, many scientists believe that what has been found in Africa seems to ==indicate== that this increase in creativity happened much earlier, around seventy to ninety thousand years ago, and more gradually. One example of an artifact that is offered to support the idea is the discovery of different sorts of ==arrows==, which would have been used for hunting. These scientists suggest that the reason more symbols haven't been found from this earlier time is because they would have been painted on things that did not survive, such as ==wood==. In fact, we are very lucky that caves have protected these specimens as well as they have, or we might never have known what these artists were capable of.

# 错题记录

The paint was made by mixing charcoal with \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ and saliva.

> 正确: fat。原文: They had fire, so charcoal was one ingredient. Another was the fat from the animals they would have hunted, and lastly, the saliva from their own mouths.
> 错误: fire
> 常见陷阱: 选项全现; 材料角色混淆
> 直接原因: 写了 fire, 火用于产生 charcoal, 实际与 charcoal 和 saliva 混合的另一成分是 fat。
> 破局法则: 熟悉题目: 按混合成分定位 Another was, 区分制作条件和具体材料。

The paint was applied using something similar to a \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

> 正确: straw。原文: They would have had to use something hollow. In fact, it’s likely to have looked like a straw, you know, like the kind we use for drinking nowadays.
> 错误: strew
> 常见陷阱: 拼写错误; 是否听音偏差需重听确认
> 直接原因: 写成 strew, 既不符合标准答案 straw, 也不是题干 a 后所需的工具名词。
> 破局法则: 完成题目: 用 for drinking 核对吸管义, 检查 straw 的 -aw 拼写。

Others say the discovery in Africa of earlier artefacts such as types of \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ disproves the theory.

> 正确: arrows。原文: One example of an artifact that is offered to support the idea is the discovery of different sorts of arrows, which would have been used for hunting.
> 错误: arrow
> 常见陷阱: 拼写错误: 单复数
> 直接原因: 写成 arrow, 漏掉标准答案 arrows 的复数 -s。
> 破局法则: 完成题目: 将 types of 与 different sorts of 对应, 核对录音的复数词尾。

It is likely that some art painted on \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ did not survive.

> 正确: wood。原文: These scientists suggest that the reason more symbols haven’t been found from this earlier time is because they would have been painted on things that did not survive, such as wood.
> 错误: wud
> 常见陷阱: 拼写错误
> 直接原因: 写成 wud, 与表示木材的标准拼写 wood 不符。
> 破局法则: 完成题目: 根据 painted on 判断材料名词, 用 wood 的双 o 拼写核对。
