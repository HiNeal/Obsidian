# 原文

(A) The group of plants known as Brassicas ==comprises== a wide range of common vegetables including cabbages, kale, broccoli, cauliflower, kohlrabi, Brussels sprouts, horseradish, rocket and mustard. Some are so familiar that they have come to be seen as rather boring vegetables. But the Brassicas provide much more than the greens on our plates, and have played their part in plant-breeding, medicine and ==warfare==.

(B) The diversity of Brassicas we see today is the result of selective breeding at different times by different cultures, but they are all ==ultimately== descended from one type of wild cabbage. The ==ease== with which wild cabbage can be adapted by selective breeding was ==demonstrated== in 1860, when researchers at Cirencester Agricultural College in the UK used simple selection experiments to produce broccoli and other cabbage-like species from wild cabbages found on the English coast. Variations in leaf form produced the tightly packed heads of today's cabbages and also other species with loose heads, while the development through selective breeding of various flower formations resulted in cauliflowers and broccolis.

(C) When the British ==naturalist== Charles Darwin came to investigate crop origins in the mid-nineteenth century, he complained that botanists had generally ==neglected== cultivated varieties. The leafy parts of plants tend to be poorly preserved in archaeological remains, but ==in the course of== the past hundred years or so, developments in areas as diverse as ==physics==, ==genetics== and ==computing== have all contributed data to the search for the origins of Brassicas.

(D) Kales, with their dark green, loose leaves, are the most ancient Brassica species. They were ==among the first== to have been cultivated in the eastern Mediterranean and Asia Minor, where they are thought to have originated. However, early Brassica ==domestication== also appears to have extended into the southern parts of China, to provide the origin of Chinese kale and the ==multitude== of oriental variations.

(E) However, kales are not well suited to the continental climates of northern Europe, where headed cabbages, in which the leaves are tightly packed together in the form of a ball, were found to be better at ==tolerating== cold conditions. Headed cabbages were apparently unknown in the ancient world, and since no ==accurate== descriptions have ==survived==, it is difficult to be sure whether or not they were known in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries. But in 1536, the Frenchman Jean Ruel wrote about a species that was clearly a headed cabbage, and its western migration started soon after, when the French navigator Jacques Cartier introduced it to Canada in 1541. By the mid-seventeenth century, headed cabbage was being cultivated in the English colonies of North America.

(F) Kohlrabi and Brussels sprouts are much more recent cultivated forms. Kohlrabi, which is ==distinguished== from other forms of Brassica by the greater width of its ==stem==, was known in late-fifteenth-century northern Europe, and was first described by the Italian botanist Pierandrea Mattioli. By the end of the sixteenth century, it was being cultivated from the Iberian Peninsula in the west, Libya in the south and across the eastern Mediterranean, but it was not found ==extensively== in England until the late nineteenth century. Broccoli has become extremely popular in recent years; today the Calabrese type, with its large green head, is the type of broccoli most frequently consumed by westerners. Brussels sprouts, of which the part of the plant consumed are the large lateral buds on the plant stems, only became widely known during the twentieth century.

(G) Many people are surprised to find that the diverse Brassica genus includes such uncabbage-like members as turnip, horseradish, rocket and mustard. This last is a clue to their common ==ancestry==: it is the chemicals in mustard oils, which were ==evolved== to protect the Brassicas from attack by insects, that give them their ==distinctive== smell and taste.

(H) One last member of this varied genus remains: oilseed rape. A crop in Europe since medieval times, oilseed rape emerged as a twentieth-century global industrial commodity for oil and fodder, and fields of rape, with its bright yellow flowers, are now common in Europe. In the early 1960s, global oilseed rape production was just under 4 million tonnes; by 2012 this had risen to ==approximately== 65 million tonnes. Furthermore, oilseed rape has become important in research being carried out in the area of plant breeding.

(I) Through generations of selection, we have changed almost every part of wild cabbages to meet our varying needs. The fact that cabbage can be developed in this way means that it is likely to remain an important food source as climates change and new means of producing food must be found.

# 错题记录

**Every kind of Brassica now available has been developed from the same wild cabbage species.**

- A. TRUE
- B. FALSE
- C. NOT GIVEN

> 正确: TRUE
> 错误: FALSE
> 常见陷阱: 正确项伪装(近义换词)
> 直接原因: B 段 they are all ultimately descended from one type of wild cabbage, 题干 every kind ... developed from the same wild cabbage species 只是近义换词, 应判 TRUE; 我被前半句 selective breeding at different times by different cultures 干扰判成 FALSE
> 破局法则: 核对三点

**Charles Darwin felt that botanists should pay less attention to cultivated plants and more to wild plants.**

- A. TRUE
- B. FALSE
- C. NOT GIVEN
